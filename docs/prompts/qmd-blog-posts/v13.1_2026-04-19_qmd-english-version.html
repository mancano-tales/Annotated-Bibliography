[STAGE 1 → PARAGRAPH-BY-PARAGRAPH READING NOTES]

You will receive the PDF of the academic text as an attachment. Your task is to generate a complete academic reading note in Quarto (.qmd) format, in English, with two sequential parts: (1) Detailed paragraph-by-paragraph reading notes, organized by section; (2) Rigorous critical analytical sheet.

Generate the output as a single code block in .qmd format, renderable as HTML by Quarto. Do not generate a separate file.

### MANDATORY STRUCTURE OF READING NOTES

After the initial block, the document must contain, in this order:

1. One `##` section per major block of the original text, with page range in parentheses → e.g., `## Introduction (pp. 1–4)`
2. `###` subsections for thematic groupings, with covered paragraphs in brackets in the heading → e.g., `### Theory and empirical contradiction [§1–§3]`
3. Flowing prose integrating content paragraph by paragraph
4. `## Synthetic Argument` → final numbered section with a callout
5. `## Critical Analytical Sheet {.unnumbered}` → see Stage 2

Paragraph numbering: Number the paragraphs of the original text sequentially from start to finish (§1, §2, §3…), across sections. Each `###` must indicate in its heading which paragraphs it covers. No paragraph may remain without a corresponding subsection.

> Ambiguity case: If the PDF has multiple columns, extensive footnotes, or interspersed figures that make it difficult to identify paragraph boundaries, adopt logical idea breaks as the criterion and indicate with `[§N – inferred delimitation]` in the subsection heading.

---

### CONTENT RULES

For each section/subsection:

- Integrate content by synthesizing paragraph by paragraph
- Expand where the PDF contains empirical details, data, examples, or citations that a brief summary would omit
- Preserve all references to secondary authors present in the original
- Reproduce relevant tables from the text in Markdown format
- Include numerical or illustrative examples when the original contains them
- Cover all paragraphs; avoid collapsing paragraphs into a single sentence

---

### MANDATORY QUARTO FORMATTING

- `::: {.callout-note icon=false}` → key concepts or central theses
- `::: {.callout-important}` → normative arguments or critical warnings
- `::: {.callout-tip}` → implications or interpretive syntheses
- Blockquotes (`>`) → for the central puzzle or research question
- **Bold** → technical concepts on first occurrence
- *Italics* → emphasis and foreign terms
- Markdown tables → comparisons between mechanisms, sources, or typologies
- Lists → only when the original explicitly lists discrete items; otherwise, use flowing prose

> Callout consistency: Use `icon=false` in all `.callout-note` instances within the reading notes. Reserve callouts without `icon=false` for the Critical Analytical Sheet, where the default icon applies.

---

### STYLE RULES

- Fluent academic English → not literal translation
- Analytical-descriptive tone: Describe what the authors argue (all critical evaluation goes exclusively in the Analytical Sheet)
- Each `###` must have at least 2 paragraphs of prose before tables or lists
- Preserve the argumentative structure of the original → do not reorder sections
- Do not number subsections manually

---

### WHAT NOT TO DO

- Do not omit the paragraph indication (`[§N–§M]`) in `###` headings
- Do not collapse sections into single sentences or bullets
- Do not invent data, percentages, or references
- Do not use executive summary language in the reading notes sections
- Do not place BibTeX outside the initial callout
- Do not add critical evaluation outside the Analytical Sheet

---

### SYNTHETIC ARGUMENT

The `## Synthetic Argument` section must contain a `.callout-note icon=false` callout with, at minimum:

- The central thesis of the text in one precise sentence
- The nature of the argument (causal, descriptive, normative, conceptual)
- What the text demonstrates vs. what remains as hypothesis or agenda
- The contribution to the broader debate in which the text is situated

  • Concise academic language in English.
